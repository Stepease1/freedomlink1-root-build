    Freedomlink1 Founders Agreement

    FOUNDERS AGREEMENT

    Freedomlink1 - Sovereign Digital Institution
    Version: 1.0
    Effective Date: March 8, 2026
    Epoch: 2 (Root Build v1.2)
    Prepared by: Joshua W. Blaz

    1. Purpose

    This Founders Agreement establishes the rights, duties, authorities, and obligations of the Founders of Freedomlink1, a sovereign digital institution built upon lineage-anchored governance, hardware-rooted trust, and cryptographic continuity.

    The Founders collectively steward:

    • the governance organism
    • the sovereign lineage
    • the Rootstone-I hardware trust layer
    • the institutional documentation
    • the public release cycle
    • the ceremonial and attestation functions

    This Agreement defines how Founders act, decide, coordinate, and preserve the integrity of Freedomlink1.

    2. Definitions

    2.1 "Institution"

    Freedomlink1, including its governance engine, lineage artifacts, hardware codices, documentation, and sovereign state.

    2.2 "Founder"

    Any individual formally recognized under this Agreement as a steward of the Institution.

    2.3 "Sovereign Layer"

    The cryptographic and procedural layer consisting of:

    • ledger blocks
    • hash manifests
    • Merkle roots
    • sovereign signatures
    • proclamations
    • epoch transitions

    2.4 "Root Build"

    A canonical sovereign release of the Institution, sealed through the anchoring sequence.

    2.5 "Epoch"

    A discrete sovereign period defined by a Root Build and its lineage artifacts.

    2.6 "Rootstone-I"

    The hardware trust processor serving as the sovereign anchor for device and civic nodes.

    3. Founders

    3.1 Initial Founder

    The initial Founder of Freedomlink1 is:

    Joshua W. Blaz
    Architect of the Sovereign Lineage, Rootstone-I, and Governance Organism.

    3.2 Admission of Additional Founders

    Additional Founders may be admitted only through:

    1. A formal Founder Resolution (Section 7)
    2. A Sovereign Signature Block
    3. A public proclamation
    4. Inclusion in the next epoch ledger

    No Founder may be added informally or implicitly.

    3.3 Removal of Founders

    A Founder may be removed only through:

    • unanimous vote of all other Founders
    • issuance of a Removal Resolution
    • sovereign signature
    • publication in the next epoch ledger

    Removal is a sovereign act and cannot occur silently.

    4. Founder Rights

    4.1 Governance Rights

    Founders possess:

    • authority to initiate epochs
    • authority to approve Root Builds
    • authority to issue proclamations
    • authority to modify governance primitives
    • authority to approve institutional documents

    4.2 Access Rights

    Founders have access to:

    • all lineage artifacts
    • all hardware codices
    • all governance modules
    • all sealed documentation
    • all CI and simulation logs

    4.3 Representation Rights

    Founders may represent the Institution publicly, ceremonially, and legally.

    5. Founder Duties

    5.1 Duty of Sovereign Integrity

    Founders must preserve:

    • lineage continuity
    • cryptographic legitimacy
    • hardware trust boundaries
    • governance correctness
    • institutional stability

    5.2 Duty of Documentation

    Founders must ensure:

    • release notes
    • changelogs
    • proclamations
    • hardware codices
    • governance specifications

    are accurate, complete, and publicly mirrored.

    5.3 Duty of Ceremony

    Founders must perform:

    • epoch sealing
    • proclamation issuance
    • signature block creation
    • lineage anchoring

    These are institutional obligations.

    6. Intellectual Property

    6.1 Ownership

    All institutional artifacts, including hardware designs, governance modules, lineage files, and documentation, are owned collectively by the Founders.

    6.2 Assignment

    Any Founder producing new institutional artifacts automatically assigns them to the Institution.

    6.3 Protection

    Founders must protect:

    • cryptographic secrets
    • hardware schematics
    • governance logic
    • institutional documents

    from unauthorized disclosure.

    7. Founder Resolutions

    7.1 Definition

    A Founder Resolution is a formal decision recorded in:

    • the epoch ledger
    • the hash manifest
    • the sovereign signature block
    • the proclamation, if applicable

    7.2 Requirements

    A valid Resolution must include:

    • title
    • purpose
    • decision
    • signatures
    • timestamp
    • lineage references

    7.3 Binding Nature

    Resolutions are binding until superseded by a later epoch.

    8. Epoch Transitions

    8.1 Initiation

    An epoch transition may be initiated by any Founder.

    8.2 Requirements

    A valid transition requires:

    • new ledger block
    • updated hash manifest
    • regenerated Merkle root
    • sovereign signature block
    • proclamation
    • public release

    8.3 Finality

    Once sealed, an epoch becomes immutable.

    9. Confidentiality

    Founders must maintain confidentiality regarding:

    • private keys
    • hardware fuse maps
    • enclave configurations
    • unreleased governance modules
    • pre-release documentation

    Confidentiality survives removal or resignation.

    10. Dispute Resolution

    10.1 Internal Resolution

    Disputes among Founders must be resolved through:

    • discussion
    • mediation
    • Founder Resolution

    10.2 Sovereign Arbitration

    If unresolved, disputes are arbitrated through:

    • a sovereign signature block
    • a formal proclamation
    • inclusion in the next epoch ledger

    11. Amendment

    11.1 Method

    This Agreement may be amended only through:

    • unanimous Founder approval
    • a formal Resolution
    • sovereign signature
    • publication in the next epoch ledger

    11.2 Prohibition

    No verbal or informal amendment is valid.

    12. Signatures

    By signing below, the Founders affirm their acceptance of this Agreement and their commitment to uphold the sovereignty, lineage, and integrity of Freedomlink1.

    Joshua W. Blaz
